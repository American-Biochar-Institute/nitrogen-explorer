# Biochar Nitrogen Explorer

**[Open the Explorer](https://american-biochar-institute.github.io/nitrogen-explorer/)**

Biochar contains nitrogen, from a few pounds per ton in wood biochar to more than 100 pounds per
ton in some manure and biosolids biochars. Most of it is tightly bound and breaks down slowly, so
only a small, variable share becomes available to crops in the first year. Biochar can also change
how much fertilizer or manure nitrogen moves below the root zone.

This is the nitrogen evidence the American Biochar Institute has extracted from its reference
library, plus 43 commercial products measured for the USDA-ARS Biochar Atlas, with the feedstock,
the production temperature, the method behind every number and the study it came from. Filters
recompute every statistic, and each figure states what its data will and will not support.

## Who it is for

Producers and agronomists, biochar manufacturers, researchers, and nutrient-planning staff who need
the underlying numbers rather than a summary. It was assembled to support ABI's comments on the
proposed revision of USDA-NRCS Conservation Practice Standard 336, Soil Carbon Amendment (Docket
NRCS-2026-0100), and in particular the nitrogen default in the NRCS Soil Carbon Amendment
calculator.

## What is in it

`index.html` is self-contained: one file, no build step, and it opens offline in any browser.

| Part | What it shows |
|---|---|
| 1. Nitrogen in the biochar | Total nitrogen by feedstock, nitrogen retained through production, and mineral nitrogen at application |
| 2. Year-one availability | What studies measured in crops or soil mineral nitrogen within about a year, and the book values those data support |
| 3. Nitrogen leaching | Change in leached nitrogen with biochar, by soil texture, study design and other factors |

## The evidence base

- 710 literature biochars with total nitrogen from 151 papers, plus 43 USDA-ARS Biochar Atlas products
- 76 year-one availability values, 57 of them plant-relevant, from 19 studies
- 743 leaching comparisons from 79 studies
- 186 published sources in all

## What the data support for the calculator

The calculator credits 10 percent of total nitrogen in year one, a value carried over from compost.
The year-one data support two numbers instead: a fertilizer credit of 0 percent for untested biochar
of every type, and a separate conservation adjustment for checking nutrient rates and risk of 5
percent for wood and crop-residue chars, 6 percent for manure chars made above 400 °C, 46 percent for
manure chars made at or below 400 °C, and 18 percent for biosolids chars. These are provisional
screening values, set near the 90th percentile of measured year-one availability where the data
allow, not maxima or validated nutrient-loss limits; some measured values exceed them. ABI's Quick Guide uses these values.

## What this does not establish

Few studies follow a biochar past one season, so the data cannot describe a multi-year release
curve. Most leaching evidence comes from short laboratory column studies, and fine-textured soils and
tile-drained cropland are thinly covered. The low-temperature manure value rests on eight biochars,
and the two highest come from one research group. USDA-ARS nitrogen methods are still being confirmed
with the Atlas team.
